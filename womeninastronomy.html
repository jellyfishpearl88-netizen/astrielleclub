WOMEN IN ASTRONOMY

Welcome to Women in Astronomy where you can look up to women who made their own discoveries and accomplishments in astronomy. Keep in mind that there are a lot of women who made successful impact in astronomy, more of them will come soon!

Caroline Herschel is one of the first women to be a professional astronomer. She discovered eight comets, 500 stars, and 2,500 nebulae in her life. (src: astronomy.com)

Maria Mitchell is the first woman astronomy professor in U.S. and also the first person to discover a comet in America. (src: astronomy.com)

Annie Jump Cannon is known for classifying 350,000 stars and developing the Harvard system of spectral classification, which is still in use to this day. (src: astronomy.com)

Henrietta Swan Leavitt was instrumental in the discovery of a relationship between the brightness and the period of Cepheid variables, a class of variable star that allows astronomers to determine distance in the universe. (src: astronomy.com)

Cecilia Payne-Gaposchkin was first person to discover that stars are primarily made up of hydrogen and helium — a finding that took years to be acknowledged by the scientific community. (src: astronomy.com)

Often called the "Mother of Hubble", Nancy Grace Roman served as NASA's first chief of astronomy and championed the development of space-based telescopes. (src: astronomy.com)
